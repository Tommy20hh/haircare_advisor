## Addendum 2026-11-23 (second review)
Research use is not redistribution. Using a downloaded copy on your own laptop for the project is one act. Uploading a copy to Kaggle (even a private dataset) is a second act: it places a copy on a third-party platform and needs the right to copy and host it. A "CC BY 4.0" label on Roboflow only helps if the person who applied it owned the copyright; a licence applied by someone who does not hold the rights is invalid (University of Reading copyright guidance). So the Roboflow READMEs prove nothing about the iStock-named photos or about the screen captures.
- **Seen in a 36-photo sample (12 per source):** `hairdata` = stock-style photos, some with faces, hands or a mirror, no visible watermark at thumbnail size (not proof of anything); `healthy_scalp` = overhead photographs of real people in a public setting (file names start with `exhibition_`), many sideways, with other people and equipment visible; `extra_dandruff` = microscope close-ups, with exact duplicate pictures (Roboflow x3 augmentation). So `healthy_scalp` carries a PRIVACY question (identifiable people, consent unknown) in addition to the copyright question.
- **Ownership question for you:** the Roboflow project owner handle is `aungkaung-phyo` (from the dataset's own README, export dated 2026-09-20) and your Kaggle username is `aungkaungphyo`: nearly identical but NOT the same string. Unresolved: the owner must confirm by logging in to Roboflow and checking whether the project is in their own workspace. Even if it is theirs, uploading stock photos (iStock file names) under CC BY does not prove they held the copyright. `data\README.md` credits `getready/hairdata-nyeix` but the dataset's own README says `aungkaung-phyo/hairdata-nyeix-wkpgb`; the paper must cite the second after you confirm.
- **Kaggle visibility:** I could not check it from here (the page fetch failed, which does not tell privacy from bot-blocking). You check it (below).
